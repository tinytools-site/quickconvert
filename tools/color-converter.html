HEX, RGB and HSL explained

Digital colors can be described in several notations. HEX is the six-digit code web developers know best, like #0d9488 — each pair of digits sets the red, green and blue channels. RGB writes the same three channels as numbers from 0 to 255, e.g. rgb(13, 148, 136). HSL instead describes hue (0–360°), saturation and lightness as percentages, which matches how humans think about color.

Converting between them is pure math. HEX and RGB are two spellings of the same thing, so switching is just a base change. HSL requires real computation: hue is derived from which channel dominates, while saturation and lightness describe color intensity and brightness. Designers often prefer HSL when building palettes, because adjusting lightness creates natural shades and tints of one hue, and shifting hue produces harmonious variations. Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between HEX and RGB?

They describe the same color: HEX writes the red, green and blue channels as hexadecimal pairs (#ff0000), while RGB writes them as decimals from 0–255 (rgb(255, 0, 0)). Conversion is just a change of number base.

What does HSL stand for?

Hue, Saturation, Lightness. Hue is the color's position on the color wheel (0–360°), saturation is its intensity, and lightness is how bright or dark it is. It is often easier than RGB for designing palettes.

Why do my brand colors look different on screen vs print?

Screens use RGB light while print uses CMYK ink — the two gamuts do not fully overlap. Some vivid screen colors simply cannot be printed. Always proof critical brand colors in their final medium.

How do I make a darker shade of a color?

In HSL, keep the hue and saturation and lower the lightness. This produces a natural darker shade of the same color without shifting its character.
