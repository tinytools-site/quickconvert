How time zone conversion works

The world is divided into time zones measured as offsets from Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). When it is 12:00 in London during winter (UTC+0), it is 07:00 in New York (UTC-5) and 21:00 in Tokyo (UTC+9). Converting between zones is a matter of shifting the same absolute moment by each zone's offset.

Daylight saving time (DST) makes this trickier than simple addition. Many regions shift their clocks forward by an hour in summer — for example, New York moves from UTC-5 to UTC-4. The exact switch dates differ by country and change occasionally, so reliable conversion needs an up-to-date time zone database rather than memorized offsets.

This converter uses your browser's built-in internationalization database, which tracks current DST rules for every region, to translate a local date and time in one zone into the matching local time in another. Enter the time as it appears on a clock in the source zone — a 14:30 meeting in Dubai, for instance — and see exactly what the clock shows in the target zone.

Scheduling across zones is where most mistakes happen. A quick rule of thumb: always confirm meetings with both the local time and the zone abbreviation or UTC offset, e.g. '15:00 CET (UTC+1)'. This tool also shows live clocks for your selected zones, handy for a glance before you schedule across continents.

Frequently asked questions

Does the converter handle daylight saving time?

Yes. It uses the browser's built-in time zone database, which includes current daylight saving rules, so conversions reflect the correct offset for the specific date you enter.

What format should I enter the time in?

Use the date-and-time picker, which produces a local date and time like 2026-10-01 14:30. That time is interpreted as wall-clock time in the 'from' time zone you selected.

Why do some places have half-hour offsets?

A few zones, such as India (UTC+5:30) and Nepal (UTC+5:45), use offsets that are not whole hours for historical and geographical reasons. The converter handles these automatically.

What is the difference between GMT and UTC?

For everyday conversion they are effectively the same — both mark the zero-offset reference. UTC is the precise modern standard, while GMT is the older astronomical term still used in names like 'GMT+1'.
