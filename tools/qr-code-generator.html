How QR codes work

QR (Quick Response) codes are two-dimensional barcodes that store information in a grid of black and white squares. Unlike traditional barcodes that hold a few dozen characters, a QR code can store thousands — a URL, a chunk of text, contact details or WiFi credentials — and any smartphone camera can read it in a fraction of a second.

Creating one is straightforward: the encoder converts your text into binary, adds error-correction data, and arranges everything into the square pattern with its distinctive corner markers. Error correction is the clever part — a QR code can still be scanned even if up to 30% of it is damaged or obscured, which is why they work on crumpled receipts and faded posters.

Common uses include linking print materials to websites, sharing WiFi passwords without dictating them, encoding payment addresses, and putting contact cards on business cards. For URLs, use the full address including https:// so scanners open the right page. Shorter content produces simpler, easier-to-scan codes.

Frequently asked questions

Is this QR code generator free?

Yes, completely free with no sign-up, no watermarks and no limits on how many codes you create.

Does my text get uploaded to a server?

No. The QR code is generated locally in your browser by a JavaScript library. Nothing you type ever leaves your device.

How much text can a QR code hold?

Up to about 4,000 alphanumeric characters, though shorter content creates simpler patterns that scan more reliably. For long URLs, consider a URL shortener first.

Will the QR code expire?

No. A QR code is just an image encoding your text — it never expires and needs no subscription. It stops working only if the encoded URL itself goes offline.
